# Payment Retry Budget and Resilience Controls

Document ID: PROD-2025-018
Department: Product
Document type: Product specification
Access level: Confidential
Created date: 2025-03-04

## Product Summary

Payment Retry Budget and Resilience Controls provides centrally governed retry limits, backoff policies, circuit-breaker thresholds, and emergency traffic controls for Payment Authorization and Gateway Adapter services. The capability reduces retry amplification and gives operators audited controls during downstream degradation.

## Problem Statement

Before this capability, each payment client defined its own retry count and delay. Fixed delays synchronized traffic across replicas and could multiply demand during database or gateway saturation. Operations could change retry settings only through deployment configuration, which slowed mitigation.

## Goals

- Enforce one approved retry policy across payment services.
- Bound the total retry demand for each provider, merchant, and service.
- Use exponential backoff with jitter for transient errors.
- Prevent retries for permanent declines and validation errors.
- Provide audited emergency controls that can be changed without a deployment.
- Expose retry behavior and circuit state through metrics and events.

## Retry Classification

| Outcome | Retry allowed | Default behavior |
|---|---:|---|
| Network timeout before confirmed acceptance | Yes | Maximum two retries with same idempotency key |
| HTTP 429 or provider throttle | Yes | Honor retry-after within total deadline |
| HTTP 500–503 transient provider error | Yes | Exponential backoff with full jitter |
| Validation error | No | Return permanent failure |
| Insufficient funds or issuer decline | No | Return decline without retry |
| Unknown outcome after possible provider acceptance | Conditional | Query status before any resubmission |

## Default Policy

The authorization deadline is 1.5 seconds. The first retry delay is randomly selected between 100 and 300 milliseconds. The second retry delay is randomly selected between 300 and 700 milliseconds. A request cannot exceed two retries, and the total request must remain within the authorization deadline.

The global retry ratio is limited to 15% of primary request volume in a rolling one-minute window. Provider and merchant budgets may be lower. When the budget is exhausted, the system fails fast with a retry-budget reason code rather than submitting more downstream work.

## Circuit Breaker

The provider circuit opens when at least 50 requests have been observed and more than 40% fail with eligible transient errors over 30 seconds. The open interval is 20 seconds. Half-open state permits five probe requests. Two consecutive successful probe windows close the circuit; any failed probe reopens it.

Permanent declines do not count toward circuit-breaker failure rate.

## Runtime Controls

Authorized operators can reduce retry count, reduce concurrency, open a provider circuit, change traffic weight to an approved alternate provider, or enable merchant-level throttling. Increasing retry count above the approved maximum is prohibited through the runtime interface.

Every change requires an incident or change reference, operator identity, reason, previous value, new value, and automatic expiration. Emergency overrides expire after 60 minutes unless renewed.

## Authorization and Audit

Read-only policy access is available to Payments engineers and Site Reliability. Runtime changes require the Payments on-call role. Alternate-provider routing additionally requires Provider Management approval when commercial routing rules apply.

Audit events are immutable and retained for one year. Secrets, payment payloads, and customer identifiers are excluded from audit events.

## API Requirements

Services retrieve policy at startup and subscribe to version changes. The last valid policy remains active during configuration-service unavailability. Policy responses include version, effective time, retry count, delay bounds, deadline, concurrency limit, circuit thresholds, and expiration.

Clients reject unsigned policy data, unsupported schema versions, negative delays, retry counts above two, or deadlines above the service maximum.

## Observability

Metrics include primary requests, retry attempts, retry ratio, budget rejections, delay distribution, circuit state, half-open probes, configuration version, and active overrides. Dashboards segment metrics by provider and merchant tier without customer identifiers.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. Fixed-delay synchronization is absent in a 500-client failure simulation.
2. Retry ratio never exceeds the configured rolling budget by more than one in-flight batch.
3. Permanent declines produce zero retries.
4. Every retry reuses the original idempotency key.
5. Configuration outage leaves the last valid signed policy active.
6. Unauthorized change attempts are rejected and audited.
7. Emergency overrides expire automatically and restore the previous approved value.
8. A 200% traffic test keeps connection-pool utilization below 75% with one provider degraded.

## Rollout Plan

The capability begins in observe-only mode, calculating decisions without enforcing them. Enforcement then starts for internal test merchants, expands to 10% of production traffic, and reaches full traffic after seven days of stable authorization success, retry ratio, and gateway latency.
