# Instant Payment Notifications Specification

Document ID: PROD-2025-012
Department: Product
Document type: Product specification
Access level: Internal
Created date: 2025-06-18

## Product Summary

Instant Payment Notifications provides customers with timely confirmation when a payment is authorized, declined, settled, reversed, or refunded. Notifications are delivered through push, SMS, or email according to customer preference and channel eligibility.

The service communicates payment state but does not determine that state. Delivery failure, retry, or duplicate suppression must never retry, reverse, approve, or decline the underlying payment.

## Goals

- Give customers clear payment-state information within 60 seconds.
- Reduce payment-status contacts to Customer Support.
- Support multiple delivery channels without coupling them to payment processing.
- Prevent duplicate customer messages during queue redelivery or worker recovery.
- Provide auditable delivery status and reason codes.

## Non-Goals

- Real-time payment authorization or settlement
- Marketing campaigns or promotional messaging
- Free-form messages written by operations staff
- Storage of full card or account numbers
- Guaranteed provider delivery when the destination is invalid or unavailable

## User Stories

1. As a customer, I receive a push notification after a successful payment authorization.
2. As a customer who disabled push, I receive an SMS if SMS is enabled and legally permitted.
3. As a customer, I receive only one message per payment state and channel.
4. As a support agent, I can see aggregate delivery status and a reason code without seeing message secrets.
5. As an operator, I can replay transiently failed jobs without creating duplicate messages.

## Supported Events

| Event type | Required notification | Example customer meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `payment.authorized.v1` | Yes | Payment approved and pending settlement |
| `payment.declined.v1` | Configurable | Payment not approved |
| `payment.settled.v1` | Yes for selected products | Payment completed |
| `payment.reversed.v1` | Yes | Previous authorization released or reversed |
| `payment.refunded.v1` | Yes | Refund initiated or completed |

## Functional Requirements

### Channel Selection

The router evaluates event type, customer preference, verified destinations, quiet-hour policy, legal restrictions, and provider availability. Push is preferred when available. SMS is used only when consent and product rules allow it. Email may be used for detailed settlement or refund notices.

### Idempotency

Each payment event has a globally unique `event_id`. The router creates a deterministic channel job ID from event ID and channel. The dispatcher checks the Delivery Ledger before sending and supplies the channel job ID as the provider idempotency key. A duplicate event must return the existing delivery result without sending again.

### Templates

Templates are versioned, approved, locale-specific, and selected by event type. Required fields include masked payment reference, amount, currency, payment state, and event time. Templates must not include full account numbers, card numbers, security credentials, internal incident details, or unverified merchant text.

### Delivery Status

Supported statuses are `queued`, `sending`, `provider_accepted`, `delivered`, `temporary_failure`, `permanent_failure`, `suppressed`, and `dead_lettered`. Every transition records a timestamp and reason code.

## Service Levels

- 99% of eligible events enter a provider channel within 60 seconds.
- 99.9% of eligible events enter a provider channel within four hours during a provider outage.
- Monthly Notification Router availability target is 99.95%.
- Duplicate delivery ratio must remain below 0.01%.
- Delivery records must be queryable by operations within five minutes.

Provider acceptance does not guarantee the destination device or mailbox displayed the message. Product reporting distinguishes provider acceptance from confirmed delivery when the provider supplies delivery receipts.

## Failure Handling

Transient failures use exponential backoff with jitter and a maximum of five attempts. Provider rate limits honor retry-after responses. Invalid destination, blocked recipient, unapproved template, and preference denial are permanent outcomes and are not retried.

Notification failure must not retry the payment transaction. Dead-letter replay preserves the original channel job ID. Analytics failure cannot block delivery acknowledgment.

## Security and Privacy Requirements

- Retrieve destination data only at delivery time through the trusted customer-contact service.
- Do not place destinations or message bodies in general logs.
- Encrypt queue payloads, Delivery Ledger records, and provider traffic.
- Limit message-body access to authorized Customer Communications operations staff.
- Retain delivery metadata for 90 days and message bodies for no more than seven days.
- Record template publication, manual replay, and provider-configuration changes in the audit log.

## Observability Requirements

Dashboards show input events, routing exclusions, queue depth and age, provider latency, success by channel, retry reasons, duplicate suppressions, dead letters, and worker restarts. Alerts must identify the affected channel and must not include customer data.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. A valid authorization event produces one eligible channel job and one provider request.
2. Redelivering the same event 100 times produces no additional provider request after success.
3. A provider outage queues jobs without affecting Payment Authorization latency or success.
4. Invalid destinations produce a permanent failure without retries.
5. Queue replay preserves the original job ID and audit history.
6. A template rollback does not require redeploying the dispatcher.
7. Load testing sustains 1,200 messages per second with p99 internal processing below 250 milliseconds.

## Rollout Plan

Phase 1 enables push notifications for internal pilot accounts. Phase 2 enables 10% of retail customers and validates latency, duplicate ratio, opt-out handling, and support volume for seven days. Phase 3 expands to all eligible push customers. SMS and email are enabled separately after provider certification.
