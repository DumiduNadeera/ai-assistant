# Payment Gateway Authorization Outage

Document ID: INC-2025-0042
Department: Payments
Document type: Incident
Access level: Internal
Created date: 2025-02-14

## Executive Summary

On 14 February 2025, the Payment Authorization service returned elevated errors for 27 minutes during the evening traffic peak. A sudden increase in card-not-present transactions exhausted the service database connection pool. Immediate client retries multiplied the request volume and prolonged recovery. The incident affected authorization availability but did not cause duplicate charges, settlement corruption, or loss of payment records.

The incident was declared SEV-1 because authorization success fell below the critical service objective. Payments Platform led technical recovery while Site Reliability coordinated traffic controls and stakeholder communication.

## Affected Services

- Payment Authorization API
- Gateway Adapter service
- Authorization Audit database
- Merchant API ingress
- Mobile and internet banking payment journeys

Settlement Processing and customer notification delivery remained available. Notifications for failed authorizations were delayed until the authorization result was known.

## Customer and Business Impact

- Incident window: 18:42–19:09 UTC
- Elevated-error duration: 27 minutes
- Authorization success rate: fell from 99.94% to 71.8% at the lowest point
- Requests affected: approximately 18,420
- Confirmed failed authorizations: 5,196
- Duplicate financial postings: 0
- Lost payment records: 0
- Estimated merchants affected: 312

Customers saw timeout or temporary-unavailable messages. Retrying after the incident generally succeeded. Customer Support received 146 related contacts during the following two hours.

## Detection

The first alert came from the authorization success-rate monitor at 18:44 UTC. Connection-pool saturation had reached 100% at 18:41 UTC, but the pool alert threshold was configured for a five-minute sustained breach and fired three minutes after the success-rate alert. Gateway latency and HTTP 503 alerts fired shortly afterward.

## Root Cause

The direct cause was exhaustion of the Authorization Audit database connection pool during a traffic spike. The pool limit had been sized using the previous quarter's peak load and did not include the traffic forecast for a newly launched merchant promotion.

When connections were unavailable, the Gateway Adapter retried timeout failures up to four times with a fixed 100-millisecond delay. Retries from multiple replicas synchronized and created a retry storm. This increased database connection demand by approximately 3.1 times and prevented the pool from recovering naturally.

## Contributing Factors

1. Capacity tests used average production traffic plus 30%, below the observed promotional peak.
2. The connection-pool alert required five sustained minutes, delaying early intervention.
3. Retry behavior used a fixed interval without exponential backoff or jitter.
4. The promotion launch was not recorded in the operational change calendar.
5. The dashboard showed total connections but did not separate active, idle, and waiting requests.
6. The database pool limit was stored in deployment configuration and required a release or emergency override.

## Resolution and Recovery

The team reduced retry concurrency, enabled temporary traffic shaping for nonessential merchant health checks, and applied the approved pool-limit override. Recovery was gradual to avoid immediately refilling the pool. Engineers then reconciled authorization attempts against gateway responses and the settlement ledger.

Recovery was considered complete when authorization success exceeded 99.9%, connection wait time remained below 100 milliseconds, pool utilization stayed below 75%, and reconciliation reported zero unmatched successful gateway responses.

## Lessons Learned

Connection capacity and retry behavior must be tested together because retry amplification changes effective demand. Business events that alter traffic are operational changes even when application code is unchanged. Recovery controls should reduce demand before increasing database capacity.
